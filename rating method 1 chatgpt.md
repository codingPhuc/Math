
## Method 1 — Write while reading + written review after + “notes based on what I learned”

**Goal:** capture the live proof flow, then convert it into _your_ structure (not transcription).

**Deliverables (what must exist at the end):**

- (A) **Margin/scratch log** (messy is fine)
    
- (B) **Post-read written review** (1–2 pages)
    
- (C) **Clean structured notes** (definitions + theorem/proof skeletons + dependency links)
    

**Steps**

1. **Pre-read (3 minutes)**
    
    - Write today’s target: “I will understand and be able to reconstruct: ____” (pick 1–2 theorems/ideas max).
        
    - Create a page with three columns: _Claim_, _Why it’s true (key move)_, _Where it’s used later_.
        
2. **While reading (Pass 1: active scratch)**
    
    - For each definition/theorem, write only:
        
        - the _exact statement_ (short, correct),
            
        - the **one key move** of the proof (e.g., “use division algorithm; minimize degree; contradiction; Bézout combination”),
            
        - any symbol conventions that are easy to mix up.
            
    - Mark every moment you _felt_ convinced but can’t reproduce: put a “?” and keep going. No stopping to perfect.
        
3. **Immediate post-read (15–25 minutes): written review (closed-book first)**
    
    - Close the PDF.
        
    - Write a mini-essay that answers:
        
        1. What did the section prove?
            
        2. What were the two or three key lemmas/tools?
            
        3. How does it connect to the next section?
            
    - Then reopen the PDF and correct only factual errors.
        
4. **Clean notes (20–40 minutes)**
    
    - Rewrite into a strict template:
        
        - **Definitions** (minimal, usable)
            
        - **Theorems** (statement)
            
        - **Proof skeleton** (5–10 lines: the backbone only)
            
        - **Dependency** (“uses: ___; used for: ___”)
            
    - Add a “Gap ledger” line at the end: list the “?” items and what you need to fix them.
        
5. **24-hour mini-check (10 minutes)**
    
    - Without notes: reproduce the spine of what you read in 10 bullet sentences.
        
    - Compare and add 1–3 gaps to drill next time.
        

**Common failure mode:** you “write while reading” but it becomes a transcript. If you are copying sentences, you are losing.
