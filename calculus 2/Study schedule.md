### Card 1 — Spivak Ch. 12 (Riemann/Definite Integral foundations)

- **Book/edition/section:** _Calculus_, Michael Spivak, **4th ed.**, **Ch. 12**
    
- **Objective:** Build the rigorous definition of the definite integral via upper/lower sums; explain integrability.
    
- **Why this chapter:** Your later techniques must rest on a correct definition (fixes “foundation” + “proof/explanation” weakness).
    
- **Prereqs to recall:** partitions; sup/inf; limits; continuity.
    
- **Key terms/theorems:** partition, refinement, upper sum (U(f,P)), lower sum (L(f,P)), Riemann integrable.
    
- **Core equations (for oral):**  
    [  
    U(f,P)=\sum M_i,\Delta x_i,\quad L(f,P)=\sum m_i,\Delta x_i  
    ]  
    Plain language: Upper sums use the _maximum_ height on each subinterval; lower sums use the _minimum_.
    
- **Worked examples to replicate:** compute (U(f,P)), (L(f,P)) for simple (f) on a given partition (use your HW “Supplemental” task).
    
- **Exercises set (from your course HW):** **Spivak 12:** #1 (skip vi), 6, 7, 8, 11; plus the **Supplemental** (U,L) problem for (f(x)=\sin x) on ([0,5\pi/6]) with the given partition. _(MATH-185-HW-PROBLEMS.pdf)_
    
- **Deliverable:** 1-page “Integral Foundations Sheet” + one 3-minute oral explanation of integrability.
    
- **Mastery check (timed; pass ≥80%):** 12 minutes: compute (U,L) for one function/partition + explain integrability criterion in words.
    
- **Estimated time:** ~5.0 h

### Card 2 — Spivak Ch. 13 (Fundamental Theorem of Calculus support)

- **Book/edition/section:** _Calculus_, Spivak, **4th ed.**, **Ch. 13**
    
- **Objective:** State FTC cleanly and explain why “area accumulation” produces an antiderivative.
    
- **Why this chapter:** This is the bridge between _definition_ and _computation_.
    
- **Prereqs to recall:** continuity; derivative definition; Mean Value Theorem idea.
    
- **Key terms/theorems:** FTC (Part I/II), accumulation function, net change.
    
- **Core equation (for oral):**  
    [  
    \frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t),dt = f(x)  
    ]  
    Plain language: If you build area up to (x), the rate the area changes at (x) equals the height (f(x)).
    
- **Worked examples to replicate:** differentiate/interpret integrals with variable upper limit; connect to antiderivatives.
    
- **Exercises set:** “Review Spivak 13 notes” as assigned in HW #2. _(MATH-185-HW-PROBLEMS.pdf)_
    
- **Deliverable:** 10-item oral drill list (FTC statements + 3 examples) recorded as bullet answers.
    
- **Mastery check:** 10 minutes: 5 FTC questions (state + apply); pass 4/5.
    
- **Estimated time:** ~2.0 h

### Card 3 — Stewart 9e §7.1 (Integration by Parts)

- **Book/edition/section:** _Calculus: Early Transcendentals_, Stewart–Clegg–Watson, **9th ed.**, **§7.1**
    
- **Objective:** Derive and apply integration by parts; choose (u) strategically.
    
- **Why this chapter:** Most common Calc 2 technique; appears everywhere.
    
- **Prereqs:** product rule; basic antiderivatives.
    
- **Key terms:** LIATE heuristic, repeated parts, tabular method (if you use it).
    
- **Core equation:**  
    [  
    \int u,dv = uv-\int v,du  
    ]  
    Plain language: Trade one integral for another by reversing the product rule.
    
- **Worked examples to replicate:** 3 standard patterns: (x e^x), (x\sin x), (\ln x).
    
- **Exercises set (course HW):** **§7.1:** #1–23 (odd), 29, 35, 37, 61, 63, 65. _(MATH-185-HW-PROBLEMS.pdf)_
    
- **Deliverable:** “By-Parts Playbook” (1 page): decision rules + 6 solved-from-scratch practice attempts (no notes).
    
- **Mastery check:** 20 minutes: 4 by-parts integrals; pass ≥3/4 correct setups + ≥3 correct final forms.
    
- **Estimated time:** ~4.5 h
