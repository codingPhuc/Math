## A) Scientific notation and scale

1. Write 0.000072 in scientific notation. Identify the coefficient and exponent.
    
2. Write 6,300,000 in scientific notation. Identify the coefficient and exponent.
    
3. What rule forces the coefficient $a$ to satisfy $1 \le a < 10$?
    
4. If $x = a \times 10^{n}$, how do you find $n$ from the location of the decimal point?
    
5. Convert $3.5 \times 10^{-4}$ back to ordinary decimal form.
    
6. Explain, in one sentence, the difference between “a power of 10” and “order of magnitude.”
    

## B) Significant figures and uncertainty (reporting honestly)

7. How many significant figures are in: 0.00340, 1200, 1200., 1200.0?
    
8. Why do leading zeros not count as significant figures?
    
9. Give the implied uncertainty interval for a value written as 3 (Zemansky-style) versus 3.0.
    
10. If a length is recorded as 12.4 cm “to the nearest 0.1 cm,” what inequality interval must contain the true value?
    
11. Distinguish precision vs accuracy in two sentences (no math).
    
12. For addition/subtraction, what determines the decimal place you keep in the final result?
    
13. For multiplication/division, what determines the significant figures you keep in the final result?
    
14. Explain the phrase “fabricated precision” with one concrete example (words only).
    

## C) Order of magnitude (what “matches the size” means)

15. Define “order of magnitude” as a power-of-10 scale in one sentence.
    
16. If $10^{n} \le x < 10^{n+1}$, what does that tell you about the “size” of $x$?
    
17. State the order of magnitude of: $7 \times 10^{3}$, $4.8 \times 10^{5}$, $9 \times 10^{-2}$.
    
18. What does “increase by one order of magnitude” mean? What about “decrease by two orders of magnitude”?
    
19. Some classes use “nearest power of 10” (rounding by the coefficient). What happens for $a = 3.2$ vs $a = 7.6$ in $a \times 10^{n}$?
    

## D) Crude estimates and Fermi bounds

20. What makes an input “crude” rather than “measured precisely”?
    
21. Why can an order-of-magnitude estimate still be useful when inputs are crude?
    
22. Estimate the number of breaths in a day using only rough assumptions. List assumptions first.
    
23. Estimate the mass of air in a room. List assumptions and give a low/high range.
    
24. Estimate how many seconds are in a semester (order of magnitude only).
    
25. If each input could be off by a factor of 2, what kind of uncertainty do you expect in a product of three such inputs (qualitative)?
    
26. Give two different sanity checks you can do after any estimate to catch a wrong power of 10.
    

## E) Bounds (interval thinking)

27. If $x \in [a,b]$ and $y \in [c,d]$ with all positive endpoints, give bounds for $xy$ and $x/y$.
    
28. What breaks if the interval for $y$ crosses 0 when you try to bound $x/y$?
    
29. If $r$ is measured with a small relative uncertainty, how does the relative uncertainty of $r^{2}$ compare (qualitatively)?
    
30. Write a sentence explaining why reporting 6 digits after the decimal is meaningless if the input was “about 2.”
