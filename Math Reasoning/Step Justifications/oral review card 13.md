### Oral Concept Prompts

Q1 [DEF, CON]  
Explain in your own words what “area between curves” means.  
Then explain how to compute it when you have two functions $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ with $f(x)$ above $g(x)$ on $[a,b]$, including the integral you would write.  
This usually looks like $\displaystyle \int_a^b (f(x) - g(x)),dx$.  
Plain-language: This integral adds up “top minus bottom” heights over the interval to get the area between the graphs.

Q3 [DEF, CON]  
Explain the difference between the **disk method** and the **washer method** for volumes of revolution.  
Describe what the cross-sections look like in each case, and give the general volume formulas using an integral in $x$.  
For example, a pure disk volume often has $V = \displaystyle \int_a^b \pi [R(x)]^2,dx$.  
Plain-language: This adds up volumes of solid circular slices with radius $R(x)$ along the $x$-axis.

For washers, we use $V = \displaystyle \int_a^b \pi \big(R(x)^2 - r(x)^2\big),dx$.  
Plain-language: This adds up ring-shaped slices by subtracting the inner hole area from the outer circle at each $x$.

Q5 [DEF, CON]  
Define the **average value** of a continuous function $f$ on the interval $[a,b]$.  
State the formula and then explain in plain language what this “average value” represents for the graph of $y=f(x)$.  
The formula is $f_{\text{avg}} = \displaystyle \frac{1}{b-a} \int_a^b f(x),dx$.  
Plain-language: This is the single constant height that would give the same total area as the original graph over $[a,b]$.

Q6 [APP, CON]  
Let $f(t)$ be the temperature (in °C) of a rod from $t=0$ to $t=2$ hours.  
Explain what $\displaystyle \frac{1}{2-0}\int_0^2 f(t),dt$ tells you in words, and why it is more useful than just taking the temperature at one random time.  
Plain-language for the integral: $\displaystyle \int_0^2 f(t),dt$ adds up temperature-over-time; dividing by 2 hours gives the mean temperature over the whole period.

Definitions

- area between curves: The quantity given by a definite integral like $\int_a^b (\text{top function} - \text{bottom function}),dx$ or $\int_c^d (\text{right function} - \text{left function}),dy$, representing the region enclosed by two graphs over an interval.
    
- disk method: A volume-of-revolution method where cross-sections perpendicular to the axis are solid disks; volume is $V = \int \pi [R(x)]^2,dx$, adding up circular slice volumes.
    
- washer method: A volume-of-revolution method where cross-sections are rings with a hole; volume is $V = \int \pi (R(x)^2 - r(x)^2),dx$, subtracting inner radius from outer radius.
    
- average value of a function: For continuous $f$ on $[a,b]$, the mean height $f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x),dx$, interpreted as the constant value that would produce the same integral over the interval.
